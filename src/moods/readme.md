# Moods

A mood (`base/Mood.ts`) is a spell over the whole world while something casts it. Every mood lives in a folder of its own, `src/moods/<id>`, with whatever only it needs, like its ambience, and `index.ts` holds one of each by its id, which is how everything else refers to them: rooms name `mood: 'bastion'`, orbs `mood: 'horror'`, and `?mood` takes any of the ids.

A mood contributes any of five kinds of transformation, all optional, all fading in and out together at the mood’s strength:

| Kind | Members | Where it applies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Color filters | `grade`, `lamps`, `fill` | `src/lib/rendering/moodGrade.ts` lays the gradient map over the finished frame after `renderOutput`; `lightLevels.ts` multiplies every light’s color by the tint of its kind. |
| Screen | `screen` | A `MoodScreen` (`base/MoodScreen.ts`) of the mood’s own, a shader drawn over the finished frame in display space, like the Paint Orb’s vignette of brush strokes. `src/lib/rendering/moodScreens.ts` builds every mood’s screen into the render pipeline once, after the gradient maps and before the dither, and runs each only while its mood shows, so a mood coming over the world compiles nothing. It takes the frame’s color in display space and returns it with the screen drawn over; the frame’s alpha stays as it is. Screens show in every graphics mode; `?show_wireframe` and `?albedo` replace the pipeline and show none. |
| Ambience | `ambience` | A centered `AmbienceLoop` class, made anew each time the mood begins, on the effects master. |
| Sound transformer | `transformer` | A `SoundTransformer` in `SoundEngine.transformers`, which every sound of the world passes through, like a room’s `reverberation`. |
| View | `fov` | Degrees the player’s own view gets wider by, or narrower where negative, like the Corridor’s 10°. `lib/MoodLens.ts` adds them to the camera’s field of view only while the view is drawn, each frame and every capture of one, so the zoom, V-viewing and the tuning panel never see them; zooming in gives them up along the way, and a camera taken from the player, like V-viewing’s, a bookmark’s or a capture’s, keeps its own. |

Two things cast moods, both asked for by `#component/Moods` every frame:

- The room the player is in, through its `mood` option, the id of a `RoomMood` (`base/RoomMood.ts`), usually the room’s own with its acoustics. Room moods come and go in 0.5 s both ways (`roomMoodFade`), so two rooms’ moods cross over evenly in a doorway, and a mood that both rooms name holds on.
- The mood cast over the world: `?mood`, otherwise the fusion’s while any portal is open, otherwise that of the prop held or viewed with V (an orb’s). In the Forge, a held magic item also casts the mood of the orb it steers the alchemy machine to: helmet → Spook, hatchet → Horror, painting set → Paint, palette knife → Impasto and pumpkin → Halloween. Picking one up there or entering while holding one fades its mood in; releasing it or leaving fades it out. Inspection alone does not activate these items. The mapping lives in `src/lib/gallery/alchemy/steering.ts`, shared with the machine. These take turns: the next one fades in only once the last one is gone.

`MoodMixer` (`lib/MoodMixer.ts`) stacks every mood showing, the room’s beneath the cast one, each at its own eased progress: gradient maps one over the other (four at most, the faintest left out beyond), screens over them in the same order, light tints multiplied, loops summed, the views’ degrees added up and transformers chained (six links, see `src/lib/audio/readme.md`). N under `?development` cuts every mood at once, the rooms’ included, and brings the ones due back in full when lifted. `?fade=false` (`src/lib/fade.ts`) makes every fade a hard cut, so captures taken right after the camera jumped to another room show it as it is: each mood shows in full or not at all from one frame to the next, the cast ones swap within a frame instead of taking turns, and their loops and transformers cut in and out along with them, as do the ambience emitters.

A screen can do more than fade with its mood: `MoodMixer` tells it every frame how far its mood has come, in uniforms it reads on the GPU, and where in the stack of the screens showing it draws, so a mood coming or going only changes uniforms. `strength` is the eased amount the color filters follow; `arrival`, the furthest the fade has come since the mood began showing, rises in step with the fade in and holds while the mood leaves, so whatever plays out along it, like strokes being painted, stays as it was; `presence` stays at 1 until the mood leaves and then falls linearly to 0, for a way of going of its own, like the strokes drying away into bristle streaks. A mood asked back before it is gone picks up where it was instead of playing out anew. Under `?fade=false`, a screen shows its end state or nothing.

To add a screen to a mood, subclass `MoodScreen` in the mood’s folder, build the frame with it drawn over in `draw`, and give the mood an instance as its `screen`; `moodScreens` in `index.ts` picks it up. To look at one without a held orb, `?mood=<id>` casts its mood for the whole session; `?mood=paint&fade=false` shows the Paint Orb’s vignette complete from the first frame.
