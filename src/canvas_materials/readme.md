# Canvas materials

A canvas material (`base/CanvasMaterial.ts`) is a kind of surface a work’s image is painted or printed on: `linen` (loom linen), `impasto` (knife impasto), `nacre` (abalone ground), `rag` (cold-press rag), `wax` (beeswax veil), `baryta` (satin baryta), `moire` (watered silk), `silverplate` (silver looking glass), `trichrome` (trichrome register), `isogilt` (contour foil stamping), `prism-sweep` (RGB exposure foil) and `intaglio-rare` (three-plate engraving). Every canvas material lives in a folder of its own, `src/canvas_materials/<id>`, with whatever only it needs, like the impasto’s `knifeRelief.ts`, and `index.ts` holds one of each by its id. `lib/canvasMaterialIds.ts` lists the ids, so the types and the WebMCP tools know them without loading any shader.

A work without a canvas material shows its image flat and unlit. `?canvas_material=<id>` sets the material for native portraits. Independently, `?new_canvas_material=<id>` sets the material for imports and successful fusion outputs, even when the fusion returns the same image. For example, `?canvas_material=linen&new_canvas_material=impasto` hangs the native works on linen and puts new work on impasto. A missing or unknown id is ignored.

Without `?new_canvas_material`, imports are flat unless `add_portrait` specifies a `canvas_material`. A fusion keeps the hung work’s material if it was imported; a native work’s URL material never carries over. The Sandbox turntable follows the same distinction for catalog artworks and external image URLs.

`createMaterial(map, size)` makes a material of its own for each image, which spans `size` in meters on its work, a turntable’s crop included: a physical node material that shades the image plane in the room’s light and mirrors the dim gallery the frames mirror (`frameStudio.ts`), and mixes that over the flat image by its `strength` in linear light, after the shading, so a faint finish keeps the image’s colors and only adds its relief and sheen. Strength 0 is the flat image to the pixel. The image plane lies around the image’s middle facing +z, in meters, so every feature has its real size whatever the size of the work, and `scale` grows them all. What a material reads from the image is measured in meters too: the isogilt’s foil and the intaglio’s grooves are as wide on every work where their contours have room, the blurs they read the picture through are as wide, and the impasto’s strokes are as long. `lib/canvasSurface.ts` hands each material the image, its luminance, the position, the view direction and `fine(size)`, which fades features out before they shrink below a few pixels. The linen (`linen/paintLinen.ts`, which the proof editor paints with too) goes further: its weave is a sum of a few waves, each filtered by how many of its cycles a pixel spans, so the threads stay as fine as the pixels can draw them and settle to their average color and relief from afar instead of beating into moiré. The rag (`rag/paintRag.ts`, which the proof editor paints with too) sharpens its felt tooth toward flat crests and hollows with steep edges by its `sharpness`, which keeps as much relief, ink and cotton as the soft tooth and never draws an edge narrower than the felt changes across a pixel, and it tilts the normal by the slope across the pixel’s footprint rather than by screen-space derivatives, which would cut sharp edges into blocks. The impasto’s knife strokes follow the picture’s contours, so they are laid for each image anew, at 1600 texels per meter, fewer on works larger than about 3.3 m², which takes about a second of work: up to four workers share it band by band (`impasto/knifeStrokes.worker.ts`), and its relief shows flat until they are done. Each stroke is drawn antialiased, its edge at least a texel wide and its bristles and lip filtered by how fine they are against a texel, into half floats, whose steps of height are too fine to light up as terraces. The shader tilts the normal by the relief’s slope across neighboring texels (`textureSlope` in `src/lib/materials/filters.ts`), which changes smoothly within a texel, rather than by screen-space derivatives, which would light every texel as a flat facet.

The two holographic finishes (`base/FoilCanvasMaterial.ts`) respond to the viewer and the strongest direct light at the center of the print in the scene drawing it. A directional light supplies its direction, a point or spot lamp its position, so its direction varies across the print. The choice follows the live intensities, lamp ranges and spot cones; ambient and hemisphere fill illuminate the print without sweeping its foil.

The materials came out of the [Canvas materials editor on Hive](https://canvas-materials.hive.lan) (`private/agent/surface-proofs`), with a slider for the strength and each of their knobs. Their fields hold the values Jaid picked there, like the linen’s scale of 0.5 at 10% strength. While a work is carried it shows flat and faint, like any other.

To add a canvas material, give it a folder with its class `<Id>CanvasMaterial.ts`, add its id to `canvasMaterialIds` and the material to `index.ts`.
