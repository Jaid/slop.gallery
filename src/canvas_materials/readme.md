# Canvas materials

A canvas material (`base/CanvasMaterial.ts`) is a kind of surface a work’s image is painted or printed on: `linen` (loom linen), `impasto` (knife impasto), `nacre` (abalone ground), `rag` (cold-press rag), `wax` (beeswax veil), `baryta` (satin baryta), `moire` (watered silk), `karakami` (karakami mica), `silverplate` (silver looking glass), `trichrome` (trichrome register), `isogilt` (contour foil stamping) and `satin-stitch` (contour-guided embroidery). Every canvas material lives in a folder of its own, `src/canvas_materials/<id>`, with whatever only it needs, like the impasto’s `knifeRelief.ts`, and `index.ts` holds one of each by its id. `lib/canvasMaterialIds.ts` lists the ids, so the types and the WebMCP tools know them without loading any shader.

A work without a canvas material shows its image flat and unlit. `?canvas_material=<id>` sets the material for native portraits. Independently, `?new_canvas_material=<id>` sets the material for imports and successful fusion outputs, even when the fusion returns the same image. For example, `?canvas_material=linen&new_canvas_material=impasto` hangs the native works on linen and puts new work on impasto. A missing or unknown id is ignored.

Without `?new_canvas_material`, imports are flat unless `add_portrait` specifies a `canvas_material`. A fusion keeps the hung work’s material if it was imported; a native work’s URL material never carries over. The Sandbox turntable follows the same distinction for catalog artworks and external image URLs.

`createMaterial(map)` makes a material of its own for each image: a physical node material that shades the image plane in the room’s light and mirrors the dim gallery the frames mirror (`frameStudio.ts`), and mixes that over the flat image by its `strength` in linear light, after the shading, so a faint finish keeps the image’s colors and only adds its relief and sheen. Strength 0 is the flat image to the pixel. The image plane lies around the image’s middle facing +z, in meters, so every feature has its real size whatever the size of the work, and `scale` grows them all. `lib/canvasSurface.ts` hands each material the image, its luminance, the position, the view direction and `fine(size)`, which fades features out before they shrink below a few pixels. The impasto’s knife strokes follow the picture’s contours, so they are laid for each image anew, which takes a few hundred milliseconds: a worker lays them (`impasto/knifeStrokes.worker.ts`), and its relief shows flat until they arrive.

The materials came out of the [Surface proofs editor on Hive](https://surface-proofs.hive.lan) (`private/agent/surface-proofs`), with a slider for the strength and each of their knobs. Their fields hold the values Jaid picked there, like the linen’s scale of 0.5 at 10% strength. While a work is carried it shows flat and faint, like any other.

To add a canvas material, give it a folder with its class `<Id>CanvasMaterial.ts`, add its id to `canvasMaterialIds` and the material to `index.ts`.
