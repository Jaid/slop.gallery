# Procedural player audio

Player effects use Web Audio synthesis only: no recordings, downloads, generated assets or additional dependencies. `proceduralAudio.ts` owns the shared oscillator/noise renderer; `playerSoundEffects.ts` owns the recipes. The same graph can render into an `OfflineAudioContext` for regression tests.

## Cues

| Catalog ID | Cue | Gameplay trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SFX-31 | Zoom Forward | Normal field of view moves toward casual zoom. |
| SFX-32 | Extended Zoom Forward | Field of view moves toward extended zoom. |
| SFX-33 | Zoom Retract | Casual zoom retracts. |
| SFX-34 | Extended Zoom Retract | Extended zoom retracts, including its automatic return to casual while moving. |
| SFX-35 | Viewing Mode Enter | Portrait or Knottingham V-inspection begins or resumes. |
| SFX-36 | Viewing Mode Leave | V-inspection is released, including target removal. |
| SFX-37 | Zoom Focus | A quiet two-tone layer sustained while zoomed. |

All seven are part of the catalog in `soundEffects.ts`, next to the 14 interface effects and the three switch sounds of `switchSoundEffects.ts` (see [Switches and arrivals](#switches-and-arrivals)). The held zoom layer’s recipe lasts a finite 1.2 seconds; gameplay holds it until released. New cues never renumber existing catalog IDs.

Zoom sweeps follow `ego-player`'s actual FOV transition direction and duration. Extended transitions use the extended family, including direct returns to normal FOV. Instant transitions produce no sweep. Reversing replaces the previous sweep with a short fade instead of stacking sounds. Explicit camera handoff, teleport and unmount release zoom audio without fabricating another transition.

`onZoomChange` continues driving the app's visual effects independently of sound settings. It also controls the quiet held-zoom layer's gain; extended zoom makes that layer slightly stronger. There is one sustained playback per zoom session, not a source allocated every frame.

## Movement

The player adapter resolves a ground modifier and passes collision-resolved horizontal speed and crouch state to `SoundEngine.step(surface, speed, crouching)`. All five modifiers retain the **Soft Weight** family—a short body impact, one filtered contact layer and a quiet delayed weight cue—while shifting its material character. `generic` is the default hard surface; `hollow` covers the wooden areas, the planks of the Bastion’s timber end by the Lodge included; `glass` applies to the walkable lobby floor window near spawn; `fabric` applies to the Sienna carpet and Moonfall crater terrain; `stone` applies to the Bastion’s flagstones, adding grit grinding under the sole twice, as the weight rolls over it, and a faint click of the heel (a landing grinds harder by the square root of its strength only, since loud grit turns into a hiss). Visible carpet bounds are shared with the Sienna renderer, and the crater uses the same configured radius as its terrain geometry. Actual speed continuously changes level, pitch, brightness and duration; per-stride pitch variation remains intentionally subtle to avoid listening fatigue. Crouching lowers level and brightness further. The stride callback controls cadence without a separate fixed footstep cooldown.

Renewed ground contact uses the exact same **Soft Weight** footstep recipe rather than a separate landing sound. Horizontal physical speed and crouch state keep the ordinary footstep timbre, while `ego-player`'s downward speed immediately before collision resolution drives a strong 2.5×–12× volume factor. Strength follows impact energy (fall speed squared), so ordinary hops stay restrained while large drops become disproportionately heavier. The factor saturates at 12 world units/second to preserve headroom. Initial placement and teleport settling are silent, as are contact interruptions shorter than 0.06 seconds. A landing suppresses a duplicate stride footfall for 0.1 seconds, then ordinary strides resume.

The reusable `ego-player` package emits `onLand` and `onZoomTransition` but contains no sound engine or gallery imports. `playerAudio.ts` owns surface selection, active-input policy, mute/lock gating and shared portrait/knot inspection state observation. Pause, blur, pointer-lock loss, mute and unmount stop held/transition audio even without another rendered frame. Narration uses the shared controller in `narration.ts` (`use-narrator` / `use-audio-queue`), with a separate output path from procedural player effects. Its meter retains paused connections for injection/resume and keys each explicitly concurrent voice separately, so overlapping `async` narration gets independent stacked HUD indicators and independent spectra.

## Mood transformers and room acoustics

`SoundEngine` has two ways into its effects master: the master itself, which the interface’s sounds play on (the pause menu’s toggles, zoom, V-inspection, the moods’ loops and the fusion portals, which bring rooms of their own), and `world`, which every sound of the world plays on (footsteps, landings, `chime`s, switches, arrivals, strikes and the ambience emitters). `world` reaches the master through the `transformers` of the engine (`transformers/TransformerChain.ts`), which every mood showing may contribute a `SoundTransformer` to: a function that wires an input to an output through nodes of its own, the dry sound included, and returns how to disconnect them again. A room’s acoustics are such a transformer of the room’s own mood (a `RoomMood` among its `moods`), which holds while the listener is inside (see `src/lib/moods`). Without any mood transforming it, as before the sound engine knew the listener’s room, the world sounds dry.

`Moods` (in `Scene`) asks the mixer for the moods of the player’s room every frame, beneath the one cast over the world, and the mixer has every mood’s transformer take as much of the world’s sound as the mood shows. The chain stacks the transformers one after the other on six links wired once, each passing on dry what its transformer leaves (`1 − amount`) and feeding it the rest before it is applied, so what a transformer took rings out on its own as its mood fades, the room left behind ringing out there while everything new rings in the room entered, and no transformer coming or going ever breaks the sound passing through. Room moods come and go in 0.5 s both ways (`roomMoodFade`), so the moods of two rooms cross over evenly in a doorway; under `?fade=false`, the links switch at once with them, while what the room left behind took still rings out. A transformer stays built for 8 s after it stopped taking any sound, long enough for any echo to die and for stepping back through a doorway to find it still there; with all six links taken, a new one replaces the one that has rung out the longest.

The mood of every room of the three worlds transforms the world’s sound through `reverberation(…)` (`transformers/reverberation.ts`): a `ConvolverNode` over a stereo impulse response generated once per context and room from what the room is built of (a predelay, discrete early reflections or a flutter echo between parallel walls, and seeded noise decaying by 60 dB at `decay` seconds while its lowpass closes from the first to the second `brightness`), normalized to unit energy, so `wet` alone sets how much of the room is heard. The Bastion rings long and dark with slaps between its close walls, the Lodge stays short and warm, the Sienna’s rug and wainscot swallow almost everything, the Oculus throws a ring back from its curved wall and Moonfall’s dome has the longest, darkest tail; the Lobby, the Corridor and the Exhibition were auditioned with them, the Antechamber, the Banquet, the Forge, the Salon, the Vesper, the Cell and the Arena follow by what they are built of.

## Strikes

Orbs and loose works sound when they strike something. Rapier reports a collision only once the solver bounced the bodies, when their velocities tell little, and a body rolling along a floor that is one collider with its walls never starts a new collision at a wall. So `ImpactSounds` (in `Scene`) runs `impactSounds` (`impactSounds.ts`, a `BodyImpacts` of `src/lib/physics`) around every physics step: before it, it takes the motion of every registered body that is awake, dynamic and enabled; after it, for every point of contact the solver pushed apart that had not touched within the last 4 steps (by its colliders, subshapes and features), how fast both sides closed in there along the contact’s normal right before, spin included. Points that keep touching, like the corner of a spinning work sliding down a wall, strike once, while a work tipping over strikes with every corner it lands on. A body’s hardest strike of a step sounds once it is faster than 0.25 m/s, at most once per 30 ms, and two registered bodies striking each other sound once. Rapier’s solver contact points are read back with the body’s pose after the step, a step’s turn off on anything spinning, so the points come from the manifold’s local contacts and the pose taken before the step. Every strike is heard from where it happened through `SoundEngine.playInWorld`: an HRTF panner at full level within 2 m and silent from 26 m on, its highs taken by distance like an ambience emitter’s.

`GrabbableProp` registers its body under its `striker`, which only `Orb` gives it so far. `orbImpacts.ts` is the glass of a 1.8 kg orb striking the ground there (`groundSurface` of the room at the contact point): a thump of its weight, a click of first contact and the glass ringing on, louder and brighter the harder it hits (speed^1.25); flagstones add grit, boards a hollow knock, carpet and the crater only thud, the Lobby’s glass floor rings back, a frame sounds like boards and two orbs clink like marbles.

`Portrait` registers every work under its frame’s `impactSound`, an id of `frameImpactSounds` (`frameImpacts.ts`) that every frame of the same stuff shares: `gilt` (gessoed and gilded wood, the default of `PortraitFrame`), `hardwood` (oak, walnut, ebony and other solid wood) and `heavy` (stone, metal and acrylic). Only a loose work strikes anything, since a hung one is fixed and a carried or fusing one disabled. The contact’s normal in the frame’s own space as it struck tells which part struck first: within 14° of its face or back, it falls flat and slaps down on a cushion of air with its corners rattling after, a side knocks fuller and a corner sharp, with the frame’s resonances and the canvas drumming behind.

## Switches and arrivals

The Banquet’s push-button switches sound mechanical (`switchSoundEffects.ts`): the light switch plays `SFX-45` Push Button On or `SFX-46` Push Button Off, the pressed button going in with a snap and a knock of the Bakelite and the other popping out a moment later, on letting the lights hum up for a breath and off letting it drop, a little lower; the light color selector plays `SFX-47` Selector Button, lighter, its detent clicking before the pop. `SFX-44` Switch Click stays in the catalog. The Forge’s alchemy machine (`alchemySounds.ts`, outside the catalog) plays its recipes in the world where they happen: an orb ticking down the sink’s throat and landing inside the body with a hollow clonk and a low gulp, the body humming and boiling with bubbles at uneven intervals while it works, and the cannon’s low thump, boom and rush of air, all kept under 1.1 kHz so it booms instead of cracking.

`ImageImporter` sounds every imported work as it arrives (`arrivalVoices.ts`), each tuned a little apart: a shimmer swells out of nothing for 0.38 s and the canvas lands with a soft thump and a warm fifth, works of one drop at least 90 ms apart, or, where the work hangs right away, the shimmer swells only until its hanging slaps it onto the wall (`hangingAnimation.impact`, 0.18 s), where the fifth rings out over the hanging’s own sound.

Every work hanging on its wall, rehung by the player or dropped onto it as an image, sounds as its hanging slaps it there (`hangingVoices.ts`, played by `useHanging` in `Portrait` from where the work hangs, through `playSound`): the swing of the arm rising into the strike, the player’s palm smacking the frame’s face with a short crack of skin over a fleshy body, the frame slapping down flat as a loose work falling flat at 6 m/s does, in the sound its frame shares with every frame of the same stuff (`frameImpactVoices`), the wall booming under the blow, deeper, longer and louder the bigger the work (by `heft`, its area against a 1.2 m square frame’s), and the frame rattling twice on its hook. Picked by Jaid from five proposals on 05.11.2026; the hook catching that dropped images played before was one of them. The palm and the wall were picked from five more on 07.11.2026, which added to it; taps as the bouncing work lands back on its wall, the hanging wire twanging and neighbors rattling on their hooks were dropped. Works hanging on the same wall within 8 m of the slapped one only jolt silently (`JoltAnimation`).

## Narration subtitles

`#component/NarrationSubtitles` shows the foreground narration’s text while its audio plays, in place of the artwork overlay, which would only repeat the same title and description. It shows one sentence at a time, or a run of short ones together (`subtitleGroups` in `subtitles.ts`, up to 56 characters), each coming up 0.2 s before its first word is spoken. Spoken words turn yellow, and the current word rises by 0.15 em through `translate`, which moves nothing else, staying up for 300 ms after it is spoken before it drops back (scaling it made it overlap its neighbors, and Geologica has no width-neutral grade axis for bolder strokes). Every letter turns yellow as it is spoken (`letterStarts`): at its own time where the timing has character spans, otherwise spread evenly over its word’s duration (an eased spread, quick at the word’s ends and slow in its middle, was tried and dropped). One path for every timing keeps each glyph drawn exactly once; a yellow copy laid over the white word, as an earlier fill did, let the white glyphs’ subpixel edges show around it. Native browser speech only reports which word it reached, so its current word turns yellow as a whole. The component reads `narrator.progress()` every animation frame and paints the words directly, so React only renders when the sentence changes.

Timings come from three places:

- Generated narrations (`PortraitNarration`) go through `NarrationAligner` right after the TTS: OpenRouter’s `/audio/transcriptions` with word timestamps (`?stt_model`, `x-ai/grok-stt-1.0` by default, the most exact in hive’s 2026-11-01 comparison of 29 systems), mapped onto the text by `timingFromWords`. It runs inside the speech cache, so eager generation and replays get it for free; it gives up after 8 s, and a failed alignment plays the audio anyway.
- Bundled narrations carry letter timings measured offline by Meta’s MMS forced aligner: `bun scripts/alignNarrations.ts` (needs ffmpeg and uv; `scripts/lib/mmsAlign.py` brings its own CPU-only Torch) writes `src/worlds/gallery/narrationTimings.json`. Rerun it when a bundled narration or its text changes; `test/unit/narration-timings.test.ts` notices stale entries.
- Audio without a timing, including every generated narration under `?stt_model=false`, is paced by `estimateTiming` over its duration.

## Ambience

Ambient sounds are emitters: subclasses of `ambience/base/AmbienceEmitter.ts` that declare looping filtered noise beds with smoothly swaying levels and scatter one-shots (`chirp`, `burst`) over each lookahead window with `scatter`, a memoryless Poisson process. Constructing one is silent; `start()` builds its graph on `SoundEngine`’s `world` (so mute covers it and it rings in the listener’s room), heard through an HRTF panner at its position, at full level within `hearing.near` and fading linearly to silence at `hearing.far`, and disabled (faded to silence) while the listener is outside its `rooms`. Distance takes its highs before its level: a lowpass after the panner closes from 18 kHz within `hearing.near` toward a muffled 2.2 kHz at `hearing.far` (`absorptionCutoff` in `hearing.ts`, quickly at first), retuned whenever the listener moved it by more than 1%, so a fountain far away softens into a distant wash instead of hissing like one at arm’s length.

World components sound an emitter with `useAmbienceEmitter(EmitterClass, rooms)`, which adds it to `useAmbienceEmitters`. An emitter’s id is the global id of its room’s ambience entry, and the gallery’s components take its `rooms` from `world.ambienceRooms`: its own room and every room whose `audibleAmbiences` in `worlds/gallery/relations.ts` lists it. `AmbienceListener` (in `Scene`) moves the Web Audio listener with the camera, tells emitters where the listener is and in which room (`hear`) and starts them once the sound engine exists after the first click. Under `?fade=false` (`src/lib/fade.ts`), emitters start, get disabled and enabled again and retune their absorption at once instead of gliding, like the moods, so captures right after the camera jumped to another room hear it as it is. `?show_ambience_emitters` draws a ball at each emitter, white at full strength at the camera, light gray when silent and blue (1%) to red (99%) in between, crossed out while disabled.

`ambience/Fountain.ts` is the Lobby fountain’s quiet trickle: a soft hiss plus rising-pitch bubbles and small splashes, audible within 21 m. `ambience/LodgeFire.ts` is the Lodge fire burning low: a steeply filtered roar (`stages` chains identical filters, 12 dB per octave each) under sparse fine crackles, audible within 14 m. `ambience/MoonfallOrbit.ts` gives the orbital sculpture a celestial hum: held tones (`ToneBed`, each optionally doubled a little higher to beat) over a faint rumble, audible within 32 m.

Moods (`src/lib/moods`) sound a centered `AmbienceLoop` from `ambience/moods` on the effects master while they hold, at the mood’s strength, several at once when moods stack, like the room’s beneath that of an orb held in it. `BastionAmbience.ts` is the Bastion’s own mood’s: cold air pressing through the iron bars in slow, deep gusts with a moan wandering in pitch at their height and a faint hiss, water dripping somewhere down the passages with its echo running after it and now and then a low whistle for a few seconds where the wind finds a bar. `FusionAmbience.ts` holds while any fusion’s portal is open, kept faint, since the portal brings its own strong sound and the mood only reminds a player far away of it: a transformer hum of the mains and its overtone, static snapping on and on in single bright clicks whose density wanders slowly, and now and then a spark arcing with a short buzz under a fizz.

## Fusions

`FusionSound.ts` is the sound of a fusion’s portal, heard through an HRTF panner at the portal on the effects master (so the mute covers it, and `AmbienceListener` moves the Web Audio listener even in worlds without ambience). It opens with the intro’s own sound (`intro.ts` in `src/lib/gallery/fusion` holds its moments): a pinprick of glass, an inhale rising over a pressure drop while the work rattles and is dragged in, a crack, a boom and a gong when the singularity bursts, a swarm of glints while the motes stream back in, the ember’s heartbeat, which latches on each ingredient on a beat of its own with a magnetic clamp charging up, a buzz rising two octaves into the beat over a sub thump and a fizz, an orb (`ItemIngredient.latch`) with a ping of glass two octaves up besides, rising through E major toward the ignition (the last pitches of `latchPitches`, one per ingredient), yanks it in with a whoosh and ignites the portal on its last beat with a short roar and an A major chord, and the portal’s slingshot: the zip of its tethers lashing out to each, a whir climbing to a howl under whooshes passing ever faster while it whirls them around, a snap and a twang as it lets go, a rush as the ring of fire they left falls in and a boom, a blast of air, a bright chord and glitter as it lands. Its peaks keep the fusion’s pulse of 1.5 s (`fusionPulse` in `timing.ts`): the burst at 1.5 s, the ignition at 3 s, the landing at 4.5 s, the bells of the four turns from 6 s on and, with the result in at once, the result flipping out at 13.5 s; the ember’s beats divide the pulse between the burst and the ignition evenly, one per ingredient (`emberRhythm`). While the portal brews, three beds run: a hum of a low fifth and its octaves through a resonant lowpass, which swells with the portal’s energy and climbs with its vortex, a shimmer trembling seven times a second and a whoosh that swells whenever a satellite passes. Every image that surfaces after the lull rings a bell with a slow vibrato, one step further along a rising motif each time; the intro’s images stay silent, as their latches sound for them. The climax rises over its whole length, the result flips out on a boom, a blast of air, a fanfare of bells and a spray of glitter while the hum breaks off, and a failure falls and sputters with a dull pop while the hum sinks an octave. Everything rings in a short, bright, synthetic reverb. `FusionPortal`’s `PortalShow` cues it from the fusion’s timeline and feeds it the portal’s envelope every frame; it only starts while the portal still brews and the sound is on.

`arcCrack.ts` is the crack of the strike that opens a fusion, on top of the strike’s own knock (`impactSounds`), from where it struck and as hard as it struck (`FusionImpact`, see `impact/strike.ts` in `src/lib/gallery/fusion`): a bolt of static discharging, with a sharp snap, crackling sparks dying away over a quarter second, a zap falling through the octaves and a sub thump rolling off into a low rumble, in a dry room with a long, dark tail, built on `EffectSound`. `PortalShow` plays it once on the world’s output as soon as the sound can play, and skips it if that is more than 0.15 s after the strike, so it never sounds apart from it. Picked by Jaid from three proposals on 07.11.2026. Rendered offline 2.4 m in front of the work, its first 0.1 s reaches about 0.11 RMS, under the portal’s inhale (0.17).

`ghostChorus.ts` is the sound of the ghosts the Spook Orb lets escape a successful fusion’s portal (`src/lib/gallery/fusion/effects/ghosts`): every ghost wails as it escapes, two detuned sawtooths and a breath sung through a two-formant “oo” that opens toward “ah” at the height of its glide and closes again, with a widening tremble, over a beating tritone, a theremin’s quavering glide and a cold draft rising with them, all in a dark 3.5 s hall. Its HRTF panner rises 2 m with the ghosts over their flight, which the beds last as long as. The ghosts’ `EffectShow` plays it once, as the result flips out, when the portal hands it an output (sound on); it cleans up after itself, so the portal closing doesn’t cut its tail off. Rendered offline 4 m in front of the portal, it peaks at 0.37 and its loudest half second (0.088 RMS) about matches the portal’s own boom (0.094).

The sounds of the other effects (`src/lib/gallery/fusion/effects`) are built on `EffectSound.ts`: an HRTF panner at the portal on the effects master, a bus with a convolution room of its own (shared per context and room), tones, filtered noise and envelopes, equal-power panners for parts heard from elsewhere, like a bat or a splat, and one timer that disconnects every node once it has rung out. Each takes the portal’s frame and the effect’s own flight data, so what it hears matches what flies: `batSwarm.ts` (a gust, a three-layer leathery flutter of sawtooth-gated noise and squeaks from where their bats are) and `bloodGush.ts` (a heartbeat’s thud and a wet gush on each spurt, three stabs of shrieking violins sinking into a cello’s groan, a splat wherever a drop lands, by `bloodLanding`) and `brushFlourish.ts` (a bristle swish from every stroke’s middle with a wet smack as the brush sets down, a plucked harp string with each, every ring of strokes a pentatonic run, and a dry rustle while they dry away). Rendered offline 4 m in front of the portal without the portal’s boom, their loudest quarter seconds reach about 0.05 RMS, below the ghosts’ 0.1, and `test/browser/fusionEffectSounds.ts` checks every effect’s sound together with the boom.

## Playback ownership

`playVoices()` returns a cancellable playback with a gain parameter. It owns only its sources, filters, envelopes and local bus; it never disconnects the caller's master or closes the caller's context. Both natural completion and cancellation disconnect nodes. Voice envelopes start with intrinsic gain zero, including before delayed onsets, so sample-boundary rounding cannot leak a full-volume noise sample.

Whatever has to happen once sound has rung out, like disconnecting an effect’s nodes, releasing a transformer 8 s after it stopped taking any sound or an ambience loop’s outlet after its fade, waits for the context’s own clock through `atContextTime`, a silent constant source that ends on time, never for the wall clock: a context rendering slower than real time, like a film’s, would otherwise cut tails off, and a suspended one would let them go before they were heard. Only the ambience loops still schedule their one-shots from an 80 ms interval, which looks 0.25 s ahead of the context’s time, so a context slower than real time only gets looked after more often.

## Films

`scripts/film.ts` records the game’s sound under `--audio`. Its page runs `installVirtualAudio` from `capture-session/script`: the `AudioContext` the sound engine makes is an `OfflineAudioContext` that renders only as far as the film’s virtual clock has come, frame by frame, at 48 kHz, so every sound starts on the frame that starts it however slowly that frame renders. It plays without the player’s click and says so through `navigator.getAutoplayPolicy()`, which `AmbienceListener` reads (`audioAutoplays`) to make the sound engine at once through `hearAtOnce`, like the intro does. While a capture poses the camera (`slopGallery.pose`), the room the camera is in is the player’s, so moods, room acoustics and the ambience emitters follow the camera from room to room. An offline context has no `createMediaElementSource`, so recordings of the narration play through it decoded (`audioBuffer` of `use-audio-queue/browser`, chosen by the narrator’s `audio.context`), on its clock, with their meter; native browser speech can’t be recorded, and the narrator’s silences between narrations still follow the wall clock.

## Validation

Run `bun test ./test/unit/sound-engine.test.ts ./test/unit/sound-effects.test.ts ./test/unit/player-audio.test.ts ./test/unit/transformers.test.ts ./test/unit/moods.test.ts ./test/unit/hearing.test.ts ./test/unit/impact-sounds.test.ts` and `bun run --cwd packages/ego-player test`.

`test/browser/worldSounds.ts` renders every strike of every surface and frame sound at several speeds, a strike near and far, the arrivals and a heavy landing through the transformers of every gallery room’s moods offline, and checks for sound, headroom (frame strikes saturate at 9 m/s to stay under it) and silent tails.

`bun test/browser/run.ts fusionSound.ts` renders a fusion that succeeds after the image model took 6.5 s and one that fails after 1 s offline, driven by the portal’s own envelope every 50 ms, heard from 4 m away, and checks for silence, headroom and a silent tail; called with `{listen: true}`, it also returns both as WAVs.

With the usual development server and existing debug browser running, `bun test/browser/run.ts playerAudio.ts` renders silent offline buffers. It checks all seven cues, speed/crouch/impact-dependent output, headroom, silent tails, held-zoom cancellation and mid-transition reversal. It does not play audio, simulate input, navigate, focus or resize the user's browser. `soundEffectCatalog.ts` is a standalone fixture that renders every catalog recipe the same way and checks for silence, headroom and silent tails. This checks synthesis and lifecycle behavior, not subjective in-game loudness or timbre.
